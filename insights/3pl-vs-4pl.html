3PL vs 4PL.
Choose with clarity.

Understand logistics execution, supply chain coordination and which questions to ask before choosing your next partner.

First, separate execution from coordination.

Third-party logistics (3PL) usually means outsourcing logistics activities such as transport, storage or order fulfilment. Fourth-party logistics (4PL) focuses on coordinating a wider supply chain, including the providers, processes and information connecting those activities.

The difference is the operating role, rather than a simple ranking. A 3PL and a 4PL can work within the same supply chain. The right model depends on the problem your business needs to solve.

Consideration3PL4PL
Primary focusExecuting logistics activitiesCoordinating the wider supply chain
Typical scopeTransport, storage or fulfilmentProvider alignment, planning and information handoffs
Working relationshipA provider manages agreed operationsA lead partner coordinates an agreed network
Useful whenYou need an activity carried outYou need multiple activities and partners to work together

When a 3PL arrangement may be enough

If your requirement is clearly defined—such as moving a consignment, operating warehouse space or fulfilling orders—a logistics execution service may be the appropriate starting point. Focus on the service scope, handling requirements, responsibilities and commercial terms.

For a single movement, begin with the relevant ocean freight, air freight or road transport requirements.

When a 4PL approach becomes more relevant

As an operation adds providers, locations and recurring flows, coordination can become its own workload. Your team may be reconciling schedules, chasing information and managing gaps between otherwise separate services.

A 4PL scope can bring those connections into a shared operating plan. Define who owns planning, supplier coordination, exceptions and review. Do not assume that a service label alone includes a particular system, integration or reporting capability.

Five questions to ask before choosing

  • Are we solving a transport problem or a coordination problem?
  • Which providers and internal teams need to work together?
  • Who should make decisions when the plan changes?
  • Which information, systems and milestones need to be shared?
  • How will responsibilities, costs and performance be reviewed?

Start with the scope, not the label.

A clear agreement matters more than a broad description. Document the activities included, who handles each handoff and how the relationship will be managed. Explore 4PL supply chain management to see the planning questions in more detail.

This is a general explanation. Provider models overlap, and the actual responsibilities depend on the agreed service scope.
